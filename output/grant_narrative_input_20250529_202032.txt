Organization: Freedom Equity Inc.

Mission: The mission of Freedom Equity Inc. is to act as a community-based lending alternative that increases access to capital for Black business owners who have historically been left out of conventional lending.

Background: – Brief history, including number of year in existence, description of activities, achievements and community impact. 
Freedom Equity Inc. (FEI), a community development financial institution (CDFI), launched in 2021 with two core goals:
1. Provide affordable capital to small business owners facing collateral, cash flow, or credit history challenges.
2. Deliver Business Development Services to applicants and borrowers committed to improving their skills, leveraging current resources, and engaging with supplier diversity networks.
Governed by a board of directors, executive leadership team, and loan review committee, our organization operates through two primary departments. Our loan department manages the complete loan lifecycle while our Business Development Services team provides comprehensive technical assistance from pre-application through the full loan term. This support includes personalized one-on-one guidance and group sessions delivered both internally and through strategic partnerships with regional organizations like the Columbus Minority Business Assistance Center, Columbus Metropolitan Library, Central Ohio African American Chamber of Commerce, and Goldman Sachs 10k Small Businesses.
The Franklin County Blueprint for Reducing Poverty highlights stark economic disparities in our region, with unemployment rates nearly double the county average in some neighborhoods and poverty rates reaching 30% compared to the county's 16.7%. These statistics informed our strategic focus on low-income and disadvantaged communities (LIDAC) tracts, where investments generate local jobs and strengthen the tax base.
Since inception, we've raised $3.81 million for our small business loan fund (25% from private sources, 75% from public sources) and deployed $2.3 million across 56 small businesses. Forty percent of our projects are located in LIDAC tracts within Franklin County and surrounding areas, with an average loan amount of $41,119. These investments have enabled businesses to purchase inventory, consolidate high-interest debt, and implement technology improvements that strengthened operations, improved cash flow, and increased revenue. To date, 349 jobs were retained and 192 new jobs created. Our impact is amplified through partnerships with the City of Columbus, Franklin County Board of Commissioners, and community organizations like the Columbus Urban League.

How does your organization determine family income? 
FEI serves a client base in which approximately 40–50% of participants are classified as low- to moderate-income (LMI). We collect this information through a self-reported income question on our program intake form to help determine eligibility and better understand the communities we serve. For consistency and accuracy, we use the HUD definition of LMI. In addition to LMI status, we also consider participants from LIDACs, using the EPA’s Environmental Justice Screening Tool and the Climate and Economic Justice Screening Tool to identify areas disproportionately impacted by economic and environmental burdens. This approach ensures that FEI’s services remain accessible and relevant to the communities most in need of support.

Goals and Strategies – Please describe the organization’s broader goals and strategies for creating sustainable community impact. 
FEI is committed to fostering sustainable community impact by addressing the systemic barriers that prevent historically underserved entrepreneurs from accessing capital and support. Our broader goals are to increase economic mobility, close racial wealth gaps, and strengthen small business ecosystems in Central Ohio in Black, LMI, and disadvantaged communities. We achieve these goals through a three-pronged strategy: (1) providing affordable capital to reduce financial barriers to starting, stabilizing, or scaling small businesses; (2) offering targeted business development services that build the knowledge, confidence, and capacity of entrepreneurs; and (3) investing in FEI’s operational infrastructure to expand our ability to serve more borrowers efficiently and equitably. By centering racial and economic equity in our work and leveraging strong public-private partnerships, FEI is helping to create a more inclusive economy where business ownership becomes a viable and sustainable pathway to generational wealth.
Examples of Success – Example of recent successes or initiatives related to affordable housing and/or small business development. 
In the heart of downtown Columbus, Parable Coffee isn’t just a cafe, it’s a movement rooted in equity. Founded in October 2020 by Ben Willis and Jeff Clark, Parable was launched with a mission to build a business that actively liberates people professionally and economically. From the start, the founders made bold operational choices that reflected their values. Notably, they opted to eliminate the traditional tip system in favor of livable wages and shared prosperity for their staff. This model prioritizes economic justice and sets a new standard for fairness in the service industry. Their staff is largely queer and BIPOC.
As two Black entrepreneurs navigating the challenges of launching a purpose-driven business during a pandemic, Ben and Jeff knew they needed capital that aligned with their mission. Through Freedom Equity Inc., Parable was able to consolidate high-interest debt into a single, affordable loan with a fixed 5% interest rate. This structure provided stability and breathing room—allowing the team to reinvest in operations, retain staff, and remain true to their equity-centered business model.
Today, Parable stands as a vibrant example of what’s possible when small businesses are supported with values-aligned capital. It’s more than a coffee shop. Parable is a community hub and a beacon of economic empowerment in Central Ohio.
